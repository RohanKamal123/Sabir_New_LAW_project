The Night Before

The Supreme Court of Bangladesh publishes tomorrow's cause list the evening before hearings, as plain HTML, with no API and no notification. Every litigator in Dhaka finds out the same way: by going and looking. This is what one barrister's week looks like when something looks for them.

Practitioner Sabir Rahman, Supreme Court of Bangladesh Sources supremecourt.gov.bd · bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd Scope Roadmap Tier 0 + Tier 1

Open the cause list and read ~60 bench lists Lists are split by division and bench, published as separate pages, with no search across them. 20–40 minutes, every working evening, done tired.
Recognise your own matters by case number The list carries case numbers and parties, not your file references. The failure mode is silent: you scroll past your own case.
Re-check a case's status by hand Case history sits behind a JavaScript form, one case at a time. Adjournments and results are found late, or by phone call.
Compute limitation on paper Sections 4 and 12 of the Limitation Act 1908 are arithmetic, but fiddly arithmetic — copy time, court closures. The one error class that can end a client's cause of action.

Thursday evening into Sunday

The journey

Diamonds on the spine are things Sabir does. Circles are things the software does while he is not looking — which is most of the value.

20:30Thu
System

The list drops. A cron job notices.

The Court publishes tomorrow's lists. A minute later barrister sweep walks both divisions: one bench-list page each, then one page per sitting bench — about 65 requests, made one at a time, 1.5 seconds apart, over roughly 90 seconds.

That pacing is the point. The whole product depends on continued access to a small government site that publishes no robots.txt, so the crawler is built to be the least interesting traffic in their logs, and carries a contact address in its User-Agent.

20:31Thu
System

Two of his matters are on it

Each listing is matched against his watches. Matching folds honorifics and spacing, because the same advocate is Mr. Md. Abu Hanif one day and Md. Abu Hanif the next. It is deliberately generous: a false positive costs ten seconds of reading, a false negative costs a hearing.

Because both cases are attached to open files, the alert can name the file rather than leaving him to recognise a bare case number at half past eight at night.

Extension of period in certain cases

5. Any appeal or application for a revision or a review of judgment or for leave to appeal or any other application to which this section may be made applicable by or under any enactment for the time being in force may be admitted after the period of limitation prescribed therefor, when the appellant or applicant satisfies the Court that he had sufficient cause for not preferring the appeal or making the application within such period.

He drafts the condonation application

He pastes in the facts and the prayer. The template supplies the cause title, the SHEWETH, the numbered grounds, the prayer in Roman numerals and the closing — that shape is fixed by convention and is not something a model should re-invent per document. The model writes the narrative paragraphs, and nothing else.

A citation appears that he did not supply. The draft still renders; the citation is flagged at the top of it.

Global legal tools with fully licensed corpora still hallucinate citations 17–33% of the time. This product has no case-law corpus at all, so the only defensible position is to cite nothing the barrister did not hand it.

One core, four ways in

Shape

Sabir touched the product through Telegram, a browser and a cron job in a single week, and never noticed a seam — because matching, diffing and limitation logic exist exactly once. A surface that grew its own copy of “does this listing belong to me?” would be a surface that quietly disagreed with the others.

Where it says no

Trust

The hardest design decisions in this product are the four places it declines to answer. Citation accuracy is the core engineering problem in legal AI — tools with licensed corpora still get it wrong a third of the time — and a practitioner who catches the software being confidently wrong once does not come back. Every refusal below is load-bearing.

“What is the limitation period for this appeal?”

The First Schedule's 183 articles exist only as a PDF table. They were machine-extracted, and the extraction is visibly imperfect — some articles still carry their neighbour's text. So none of them is trusted.

UnverifiedRuleError: Schedule article 152 has not been verified by a lawyer. Check it against bdlaws and set "verified": true.

“Find me authority for this proposition.”

DLR, BLD, BLC, BLT and MLR are paywalled with no public API. There is no corpus, so there is no grounding, so there is no answer — and the drafting prompt forbids citing anything the barrister did not supply.

[AUTHORITY TO BE SUPPLIED]

“Fill in the facts I didn't give you.”

A drafting model that invents a date, a sum or a procedural step produces a document that reads perfectly and is wrong. Gaps are marked, never closed.

[TO BE SUPPLIED: date of service of the impugned notice]

“Was this delay condonable?”

Section 5 turns on “sufficient cause”, which is judgment, not arithmetic. The calculator reports the period as expired and stops there, with a pointer to the section.

If this period has expired, s. 5 may still permit admission on sufficient cause — that is a matter of judgment, not computation.

What is still open

Seams

Honest status. These are not future features — they are things this build depends on that a human still has to do.

93 limitation articles await verification

The extractor and the review queue exist (barrister review-queue); the reading does not. Until an article is checked and flipped, the calculator refuses it. This is knowledge-encoding work only a lawyer can do.

Terms of use on both sites have not been read

Neither publishes a robots.txt — that was checked, and both return 404. That is the absence of a machine-readable restriction, not the presence of permission. Reading the published terms, and emailing the Court's IT contact before running the sweep on a schedule, is outstanding.

Bar Council rules on solicitation are unchecked

Everything built so far is inward-facing practice software — a barrister's own list, own cases, own files — which keeps clear of the obvious problem. That is a description of the code, not a legal opinion, and it stops being sufficient the day this is sold to other chambers.

The web UI has no authentication

Single-tenant by design; bind it to localhost or put it behind an authenticating proxy. The schema is already per-user throughout, so adding login later is a form, not a migration.

Barrister Tools — Roadmap Tier 0 and Tier 1, built as one product. 309 tests, none of which touch the network: every parser runs against real pages saved from the live sites, so a change to the Court's markup is caught against what it actually serves rather than against a mock.
